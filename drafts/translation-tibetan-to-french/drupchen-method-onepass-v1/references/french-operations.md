# Tibetan devices and the French operation for each

From Drupchen, *Two strategies for improving machine translation from Tibetan* (2026), §4 and §4.1, CC BY 4.0. Lightly reformatted. **Calibrated for French.** For another target language these rows are hypotheses, not results.

The table helps less than it looks: a rule is often acknowledged and then overridden by the model's default. Use it together with the correction examples, if you have any.

## The nominalisation cascade (§4.1)

When the source stacks nominalised complements under one copula (*that is the cause of [X], [Y], [Z]*), French resists: three or four *le fait de…* under *c'est la cause de* read like administrative prose. So each complement becomes a finite clause; a finite clause needs a tense; the only tense that fits a consequence is the future; a future needs a subject, and the one available is *vous*. Four forced steps, and a description has become a promise.

The repair is one operation: one predication, the complements as infinitival subjects, present tense, no addressee subject. For example: *Entendre ces mots ferme la porte des renaissances inférieures ; en pratiquer le sens apaise la souffrance du karma et des émotions perturbatrices ; en garder ne fût-ce que quatre vers libère de l'océan du samsara.*

## Colophons

A colophon is one head-final period: occasion or request, the requester's honorific phrase, place, date, the author's humble epithet, aspiration, then the verb with its agent marked late (§9.3). In French:

- Put the main verb early (*Ce texte a été composé par …, à la demande de …*). Do not keep it for the end of a sixty-word sentence.
- Re-order the slots for French. The Tibetan order is a convention, not logic.
- Keep the author's epithet as a phrase attached to the name. Do not make it a sentence of its own.
- Do not add *ainsi*, *donc*, *c'est pourquoi* or similar connectives that no Tibetan particle licenses.
